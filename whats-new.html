Isotope production (2.0)

Where: build an isotope laboratory, then open MATERIALS in the top bar.

Until now electricity could only be sold. An isotope laboratory spends it on isotopes instead, which you can keep or sell for Flux. How the isotopes are made is kept abstract: the game shows what they are used for, not how real ones are produced.

The laboratory

  • Research Applied isotope production (ϟ25,000, 10 minutes, after Commercial reactor design), then build the laboratory for ϟ25,000. Up to 2 on each floor.
  • It makes one recipe at a time. Pick a recipe while it is idle, then Start. Pause stops it drawing and keeps the batch; Stop throws the batch away with no refund. Repeat on starts the next batch by itself.
  • Its panel shows the batch's progress, what a batch sells for now, what the same electricity would sell for on this floor now, and the difference.

Iodine-131 also needs a reactor running on the same floor. Each recipe has a short note on what the isotope is used for, and the Library has a page on isotopes.

Where the electricity comes from

  • A laboratory takes electricity from its own floor's storage: after cooling towers, before research and automatic exports.
  • If there is not enough, it slows down and says Short of electricity, then carries on by itself when there is more. Storage never goes below zero.
  • A batch always costs the same electricity, however slowly it gets it.

MATERIALS: keeping and selling

  • Every floor's isotopes go into one shared stock. MATERIALS lists each one with its stock, price and value, and the whole collection's value.
  • Sell 1, Sell 10 or Sell all. Each button shows the Flux it pays before you press it. Prices move a few per cent once a minute.
  • Isotope sales are not electricity sales: they do not count towards export objectives, the programme or Legacy points.

Is it worth it?

While your exports keep up with the plant, isotopes earn a little more than selling the electricity, never a lot more. Each recipe has its own use (since 2.0.2):

  • Iodine-131 earns the most when there is no electricity to spare, at any price and on any floor, but only while a reactor on its floor runs.
  • Carbon-14 is small and safe: on the mainland it beats exporting at any price, it needs no reactor and draws only 4 E/s, so it earns little.
  • Cobalt-60 is the best use of a modest surplus, about 8 to 23 E/s that would otherwise be wasted; without one it roughly breaks even.
  • Molybdenum-99 loses money unless electricity is being wasted, and is by far the best when a lot is: storage full and more made than the export limit can sell.

Reactor operating modes

Where: click any reactor, then OPERATING MODE in its panel.

Every reactor now runs in one of three modes. The mode is separate from the power slider: power says how hard the reactor is pushed, the mode says how it trades electricity against heat.

Economy×0.85 electricity · ×0.70 heatLess output, much cooler. The best choice with no cooling, and the safest.
Standard×1 electricity · ×1 heatThe best choice with one cooling tower. Exactly how reactors worked before 1.9.
High output×1.15 electricity · ×1.40 heatMore output at the cost of much more heat. Worth it with heavy cooling.

How heat works with a mode

  • A reactor drifts towards a resting temperature over about 40 seconds: 20 + 40 × power² × mode heat, then minus cooling. The mode only changes the heat the reactor makes, never the 20° it starts from.
  • Output is base × power × mode electricity × efficiency. So 120% power in High output gives base × 1.20 × 1.15 = ×1.38 before temperature.
  • Temperature still costs output: full efficiency up to 70°, 95% at 84° and 85°, 65% at 99°, in straight lines between. At 100° the reactor shuts down and makes nothing until it cools below 60° and you restart it.
  • Each reactor keeps its own mode, and the mode is saved. New reactors start in Standard.
  • The panel shows the mode's description, its electricity and heat multipliers, where the temperature will settle, and the efficiency right now.

Worked example

A small commercial reactor with one MK-I cooling tower (15° of cooling). In Standard at 140% it settles at 20 + 40 × 1.96 − 15 = 83°, runs at 95% efficiency and makes ×1.33 of normal. In High output the best it can do with the same tower is 120%: it settles at 20 + 40 × 1.44 × 1.40 − 15 = 86°, just into Overheating, at 94% efficiency, for ×1.29. With one tower, Standard wins. Without any cooling, High output at 120% would settle at 101° and shut down.

Which mode is best? (balance check)

The best power level for a small commercial reactor, found in 5% steps without ever shutting down, after the towers' own electricity. Figures are output as a multiple of the reactor's normal rate; every other reactor comes out within a few hundredths of these.

  • Economy is best with no cooling, and the safest: it can sit at 150% forever without a shutdown, even if its towers lose power.
  • Standard is best with one cooling tower of any level.
  • High output is best only with heavy cooling, such as two MK-III towers. Without cooling it shuts down above about 119%.
  • Why it works out this way: at the same temperature, output is in proportion to the mode's electricity divided by the square root of its heat. That comes to Economy 1.016, Standard 1.000 and High output 0.972, so High output pays for its extra electricity, and Economy only loses when it hits the 150% limit.

Scientific Development Programme

Where: the small tracker above Construct, bottom left. Click its title to open the programme; the − button folds the objective list away.

Five stages that guide a run from the first hand-split atom to a plant on several floors. Each stage has three objectives. When all three are ticked you press Complete and get that stage's reward, which lasts until you begin again with Scientific Legacy.

The rules

  • Electricity counts when it is generated into storage. Sales count only real electricity sales; money from Experimental Ventures never counts.
  • The 125% objective tracks the longest unbroken spell this run with any reactor at 125% or more; a shutdown ends the spell.
  • Rewards are added once to the bonus sum, beside technologies, discoveries and Legacy. A stage can't be claimed twice.
  • The programme screen shows the strip of five stages, each objective with a progress bar and what it is counted over, the reward, what the next stage asks for, and every bonus active this run. The stage you just finished and its reward light up briefly.
  • Scientific Legacy starts the programme again at Stage 1 and removes its rewards, so they can be earned again. The best stage you ever completed is kept as a record only and gives nothing, so there is nothing to farm.
  • Every objective is reachable in order: Stage 2 can be done without a reactor (two Advanced auto splitters make 11.2 E/s), and no stage needs something that only unlocks in a later one. The targets were checked against the game's economy and none needed changing.

Unified research network

Where: any laboratory on any floor. Its panel now shows the whole network.

Before 1.9 each floor's labs ran their own single project. Now every lab in the plant works on one shared list of projects, and every floor sees the same list.

Project slots

Labs in the plant (any floors)Projects at once
1 or 21
3 to 52
6 or more3

You can still build up to 5 labs on each floor, so three slots are within reach.

How fast research goes

  • Each floor's labs work out their speed exactly as before, at that floor's own rate, and the network adds the floors together.
  • Local bonuses stay local: Corran Plateau's +25% research, and the observatory beside a lab, speed up only the labs on Corran.
  • Plant-wide bonuses (technologies, Legacy, discoveries, the programme's +10% research) apply to every floor's labs once, so nothing counts twice.
  • One project gets the whole network's speed. Two or three projects share it evenly, so total research per second is the same as before. More slots let projects run side by side; they don't make research faster.
  • Technology costs and research times are unchanged.
  • Away from the game, every floor's labs push the projects at 50%, once, as before.

What the lab panel shows

Labs in total, projects running out of slots, network speed, each running project with its progress and time left, and each floor's labs and rate. The Experimental Ventures tab is still there next to the tree.

Automatic reactor control

Where: a reactor's panel, AUTOMATIC CONTROL. Unlocks with High-flow cooling, a technology that was already in the game.

Fit a controller to a reactor, switch it on and pick a target temperature. It moves the power slider for you to hold that temperature.

Price: 20% of the reactor, at least ϟ1,000

ReactorFloorController

How it behaves

  • Target from 40° to 75°, 65° to start.
  • It moves power one 5% step at a time, no faster than one step every 2 seconds, towards the highest setting that settles at or below the target. It allows for the cooling, mode and heat bonuses the reactor has at that moment.
  • It steers by where the temperature will settle, not where it is now, so it doesn't overshoot or swing back and forth. The one exception: at 85° or more (Overheating) it steps down regardless.
  • It stays between 25% and 150%. If even 150% can't reach the target, it just runs at 150%.
  • It never restarts a reactor after a shutdown. It waits and shows Cooling after shutdown; the restart button is always yours.
  • Moving the power slider by hand switches it off at once. Other reactors are not affected.
  • Every reactor has its own controller, on every floor. It works while you are away too, inside the usual 25% electricity and 8-hour limit.

What the panel shows

Fit, on/off and the target, plus target, power, temperature and one status:

Increasing outputReducing outputHolding temperatureCooling after shutdownManual

How they work together

  • Automatic control knows about operating modes and cooling towers, so it holds the target in Economy, Standard or High output, with or without a tower.
  • The programme's +10% research reward speeds up the whole research network by exactly 10%.
  • Technologies finished by the network count towards programme objectives.
  • Programme rewards stack with discoveries and Legacy in one bonus calculation, each applied once.
  • Scientific Legacy clears every floor as before, so modes and controllers go with the reactors. It also clears the programme rewards and the research projects. Rare and better discoveries stay, as before.
  • Time away: all three floors, every controller and the shared research list keep going while the game is shut, at the usual rates.

Older saves

  • Saves from 1.9 load into 2.0 with an empty isotope stock. Saves from before 1.9 load too. Nothing is lost or refunded.
  • Reactors load in Standard with no controller.
  • Each floor's old research project joins the shared list with its progress. If two floors were researching the same technology, it becomes one project, keeping whichever was further on. If more projects are running than your slots allow, they all finish; the limit only stops you starting new ones.
  • The programme starts at Stage 1 with every objective read from the save. Electricity generated is estimated from what was sold plus what is in storage, so it never claims more than really happened. The 125% spell was never recorded, so that one has to be run.
  • Export save and Import save carry everything new: modes, controllers, targets, the programme, the research list, the laboratories and the isotope stock.

How it was tested

Each system was built, tested and saved separately, one after another, then all were checked together.

Automated checks: 537 of 537 passing

These cover the rules, saving and loading, old-save migration, and the combinations above, including eight hours away on three floors with every reactor in High output with a tower and a controller: none shut down, all ended at or under target.

Clicked through in a real browser

  • Switching a reactor to High output raised it from 23.30 to 26.79 E/s (in 1.9.0, before High output ran hotter).
  • Stages 1 and 2 played on a new save with real clicks. Stage 1 paid exactly ϟ100 and did not count as sales; Stage 2 gave exactly +5%; a second claim did nothing.
  • The research panel on a three-floor plant: Corran's labs ran at 5.21 against Seren's 2.99, as the local bonuses say they should.
  • A controller fitted for ϟ7,100 held a target of 65° at 105% power, settling at 61.9°. Moving the slider switched it to Manual and left the other reactors alone.
  • Dragging a building to move it, clicking to select it, picking Ruben up and flinging him, and exporting a save and importing it back.
  • The one-file game and the web copy both start with no errors.
  • 2.0: building a laboratory, choosing and starting a recipe, running out of electricity and carrying on, finishing a batch, selling, a second laboratory on another floor, switching floors, saving and reloading, and the away card.
  • 2.0.1: the top bar and panels at 320, 360, 375, 390 and 500 pixels wide.

Known issues and what hasn't been tested

  • In 1.9.0 Standard mode was never the single best choice. A balance check found it, and 1.9.1 fixed it by making High output run hotter (section 2).
  • Older saves with a reactor left in High output at a power that now shuts it down have that reactor stepped down once, on loading, to the highest power that settles no hotter than before.
  • Nobody has yet played from an empty plant all the way through Stages 3 to 5; those were checked with tests and the ready-made plants.
  • Checked at phone widths in a desktop browser, not on a real phone.
  • Molybdenum-99 is still very strong when a lot of electricity would otherwise be wasted. That is the value of not wasting it, and is left as it is.
